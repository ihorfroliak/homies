| D-43 | 2026-09-20 | **Застосунок підключається до БД роллю, яка не є власником таблиць і не має UPDATE/DELETE на append-only таблицях** (`ops/sql/app_role.sql`, роль `homies_app`). Перевіряється на старті у production-подібних середовищах (`verify_ledger_privileges`), як SEC-02 перевіряє секрети | Append-only тримали лише тригери (D-04), а тригер не зв'язує власника таблиці: `ALTER TABLE journal_lines DISABLE TRIGGER ...` — і леджер редагується вільно, потім тригер вмикається назад. Перевірка на старті, а не запис у ранбуці, бо крок провіженінгу, який ніхто не контролює, — це крок, який одного разу не виконають, і помітно це стане того дня, коли леджер мусить бути доказом. TRUNCATE відкликається окремо: він не викликає рядкових тригерів узагалі, тож наданий помилково спорожнив би леджер, не потривоживши жодної гарантії | Settled |

| D-41 | 2026-08-17 | **Chargebacks follow the money, not the outcome, and the platform absorbs a post-payout loss.** Entries post at `charge.dispute.created` (the amount and the provider fee both leave then); a win reverses the amount but never the fee; a loss posts nothing. If the booking was still unpaid the dispute discharges `booking_escrow`; if it was already paid out the amount lands in a new `chargeback_loss` expense account. `charged_back` is a distinct payment and booking state, not reused `refunded`/`cancelled` | Money leaves the provider balance at *creation* while the outcome is unknown, so entries keyed to resolution would misstate cash for months. Platform absorption is not a policy preference but the only truthful entry available: there is no clawback mechanism (FIN-02) and no host agreement to invoke — the agency contract is still being drafted — so booking a host receivable would assert a claim that does not exist. Recovery later is a compensating entry, which the append-only ledger already requires. The states are distinct for the same reason `expired` is not `cancelled` (D-22): a guest who asked for their money back and a guest who went to their bank mean different things, and only the second can cost the business its card processing | Settled |
| D-40 | 2026-08-05 | **Monetary KPIs are ledger queries, in integer minor units, over half-open windows — and the endpoint declares what it cannot answer.** Commission is read from `payout_allocated`, never derived as `gmv * fee_bps`; ratios are returned in basis points, never floats; currency is a mandatory filter | The counterpart to D-38: money stays out of Prometheus, so this is where the KPI framework's monetary rows get answered. `bookings.total_amount` survives a refund untouched, so summing it reports money the business gave back — the ledger is append-only and reconciled, and is the only defensible source. Currency is mandatory because an unscoped sum is exactly the H1 defect in reporting clothing. Half-open `[from, to)` because a boundary day counted in both December and January is the classic reporting bug. Commission is *recognised* at payout, not at capture: a paid booking whose stay has not happened has earned the platform nothing, and the guest can still cancel — deriving it from GMV books revenue the business may have to give back. The `unavailable` list ships in the payload because six numbers look complete: without it the founder cannot tell that CM2 is missing for lack of cost data rather than being zero | Settled |
| D-39 | 2026-08-04 | **Alert rules are tested code, and nothing may alert at zero traffic.** 11 alerts in `ops/monitoring/rules/`, each with a `for:` window, each covered by a `promtool test rules` case in CI — 12 of those cases assert an alert stays *silent*. Absence alerts ("no bookings in 24h") are deliberately excluded | The usual alert-rule bug is a metric name that never existed, found during the outage the rule was written for; promtool validates syntax but not whether a series is ever produced, so a pytest also cross-checks every `homies_*` name in the rules against what the app actually exposes (it caught two seeded typos). The zero-traffic constraint matters more: this business has not launched, so an absence alert would fire continuously, get muted, and take the one real page down with it. | D-38 | 2026-08-04 | **Money never becomes a Prometheus counter.** OBS-03 counts *events* (registrations, bookings by outcome, payments succeeded/failed/refunded/voided, payouts); GMV, commission, net revenue and take rate stay ledger queries. Counters are also queued and flushed on `after_commit`, never at the transition | A counter resets on restart, is only observed at the scrape interval, and cannot be recomputed for a past window. This system already has a financial source of truth — an append-only ledger, checked by invariant I5 and reconciled to zero — and a GMV counter would be a second, lossy record that disagreed with it after the first restart, at which point neither could be trusted. Event *counts* have none of those properties and are what alerts are written against. The commit gating exists for the same reason a counter cannot be repaired: services mutate state but the router commits, so incrementing at the transition would permanently count a payment that raced and lost as a success | Settled |
| D-37 | 2026-08-04 | **`/healthz` stays dependency-free (liveness); the database check goes in a new `/readyz` (readiness).** The probe reports the exception *class name* only, never its message | The audit records OBS-01 as "`/healthz` does not check the database", but implementing that literally builds an outage amplifier: a failing liveness probe makes an orchestrator **restart** the container, so one database outage would restart every replica at once and stampede the database with cold starts on recovery. A failing readiness probe instead **removes the instance from the load balancer and leaves it running**, so it rejoins by itself. The same audit line names the real gap — "no readiness/liveness split" — and that is what was built. Error messages are redacted because `/readyz` is unauthenticated and rate-limit exempt, and a SQLAlchemy connection error stringifies the DSN, password included | Settled |
| D-36 | 2026-08-04 | **Supply-chain gates audit only what this repo declares.** `pip-audit .` (resolves pyproject) rather than a bare `pip-audit` over the environment; coverage `fail_under` set at **80** against a measured **84.2%** branch floor, with nothing excluded to flatter it; Dependabot **monthly and grouped**, with `ruff`/`mypy` ignored | A gate that fails for reasons outside the repo trains people to ignore it. A bare `pip-audit` reports CVEs in the runner's own `pip` — measured: 5, all in pip, runtime deps clean — so a new pip CVE would redden a green build with no code change, the same failure mode as an unpinned linter (D-26). | D-35 | 2026-08-04 | **mypy is a blocking CI gate**, at default strictness over `app/` only, pinned to `mypy==2.3.0`. | D-34 | 2026-08-03 | The payment-provider call runs **after** the booking transaction commits, never inside it. A booking may briefly exist without a payment; a replay of the same `Idempotency-Key` heals it, and the TTL frees the dates if it never does | The provider call is network I/O and used to sit between `SELECT listing FOR UPDATE` and `commit()`, so every concurrent booking of that listing queued behind one Stripe round-trip and a pooled connection stayed pinned for its duration. Creating the intent *before* the lock was rejected: losing the date race would then leave an orphan intent at Stripe — exactly what reconciliation flags. Post-commit keeps the stronger invariant: **no intent without a committed booking**. Safe to retry because the Stripe idempotency key is the booking id (D-33) | Settled |
| D-33 | 2026-08-03 | The payment provider receives an **opaque `idempotency_key`** from the caller; it never generates one. The booking id is the scope (one booking owns exactly one intent — `payments.booking_id` is unique) | The adapter built `pi_{host}_{amount}_{currency}_{uuid4()}`, so every call was a fresh key and Stripe's idempotency protected nothing: a retry after a failed transaction created a second intent for the same booking — a double charge. Passing an opaque key rather than a `booking_id` keeps the Stripe seam free of domain concepts (ADR-0007); the `pi_`/`re_` prefix stays in the provider as the operation marker | Settled |
| D-31 | 2026-08-03 | The raw Stripe webhook event is committed in **its own transaction before dispatch**; an unprocessed row (`processed_at IS NULL`) is the dead-letter marker | It used to be `flush()`ed in the same transaction as the ledger handlers and committed only at the end, so any handler failure rolled the audit record back with it — reproduced: unknown intent → 404 and **zero** `webhook_events` rows. Two transactions cost one extra commit; losing the record of money events costs reconciliation itself. No new column is needed: "persisted but not processed" already means dead-letter | Settled |
